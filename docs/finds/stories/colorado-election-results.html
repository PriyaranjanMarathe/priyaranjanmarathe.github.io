§ 1

What each party actually did

Democrats won the statewide offices, the U.S. Senate race and seven U.S. House seats, while several conservative citizen initiatives also passed. The candidate results and the policy results tell different stories.

Democrats

Statewide offices, congressional gains, legislative supermajorities
  • Held all four statewide offices: Governor, Attorney General, Secretary of State and Treasurer.
  • Re-elected Sen. John Hickenlooper.
  • Flipped three Republican U.S. House seats: CD-4 (Eileen Laubacher), CD-5 (Jessica Killin) and CD-8 (Manny Rutinel), changing the delegation from 4–4 to 7–1 Democratic.
  • Gained seven legislative seats: +1 in the Senate (24–11) and +6 in the House (49–16), reaching the two-thirds threshold in both chambers.
  • Held their existing board seats: all three CU Regent seats and both Democratic-held State Board of Education seats on the ballot. Education District 3 remains unresolved in the cited results.
  • On measures: Amendment 87’s graduated income tax, Proposition NN’s revenue retention and Proposition 137’s conservation funding were approved.

Republicans

Lost statewide races, won several policy fights
  • Lost all four statewide offices and the U.S. Senate race.
  • Three House incumbents defeated: Lauren Boebert in CD-4, Jeff Crank in CD-5 and Gabe Evans in CD-8. Jeff Hurd held CD-3.
  • Lost seven legislative seats, leaving Democrats with enough seats for veto overrides and legislative referrals of constitutional amendments if their caucuses vote together.
  • Four conservative propositions approved: 132 (fentanyl penalties), 133 (trafficking of minors), 134 (school sports eligibility) and 135 (surgery on minors to alter sex characteristics).
  • All six Republican-backed constitutional amendments were rejected: 81–86. Proposition 136’s 4.4% income-tax cap was rejected too.

§ 8

Certification — and what happens next

A race call and official certification are different steps. The signature-cure and military/overseas ballot deadline has passed, but county canvasses, recount determinations and statewide certification still follow. A mandatory recount applies when the margin is no more than 0.5% of the highest vote total in that contest, including a Yes/No question. It is not 0.5% of all votes cast.

§ 9

Questions & answers

The parts of the results that do not explain themselves.

Amendment 85 had more Yes than No votes. Why was it rejected?
Because it needed 55% Yes, not a simple majority. Its published unofficial tally is 1,340,988 Yes to 1,281,060 No: 51.14% Yes, or 3.86 percentage points below the constitutional threshold. A 59,928-vote Yes lead did not satisfy that rule.
Why did Amendment 87 need only a majority?
Colorado’s 55% rule has an exception for amendments limited to repealing constitutional language. The official Blue Book says Amendment 87 needs a simple majority. Its 51.64% Yes cleared that bar, while Amendment 85’s 51.14% did not clear 55%. Similar vote shares produced opposite outcomes because the thresholds differed.
Which approved measure had the smallest Yes–No vote gap?
Amendment 87, at 86,151 votes (51.64%–48.36%). Next are Proposition 137 at 99,634 and Proposition 135 at 101,080. These are computed from Ballotpedia’s published unofficial counts. Amendment 85’s smaller Yes–No gap does not make it approved: it needed 55%. Proposition 133 had the highest Yes share, 62.12%; Amendment 84 had the lowest, 36.64%.
Did Lauren Boebert lose?
Yes. AP called CD-4 for Eileen Laubacher on Nov. 13, and Boebert congratulated her. The race is no longer uncalled. This Democratic pickup makes Colorado’s delegation 7–1 Democratic; election certification is still pending.
How did Democrats win the statewide offices while four conservative propositions passed?
Candidate contests and ballot measures are separate choices. Propositions 132–135 each won majority support, while Democrats won all four statewide offices and the U.S. Senate race. All six Republican-backed constitutional amendments, 81–86, were rejected, as was Proposition 136’s income-tax cap. The aggregate results show a mixed policy outcome, not how any individual voter filled out a ballot.
What does a legislative supermajority let Democrats do?
If they vote together, Democrats have the seats to override a governor’s veto and refer constitutional amendments to voters. Both require two-thirds support in each chamber: 24 of 35 Senate seats and 44 of 65 House seats. MultiState reports Democrats at 24 and 49 respectively. This provides a legislative route to the ballot; the citizen-initiated Amendments 81–87 used a different route.
Are these results final?
They are latest published unofficial results, not certified totals. County canvasses are due Nov. 25, with statewide certification scheduled for Nov. 30. Recounts can produce later revisions. Ballotpedia does not display an exact tally-update timestamp for the statewide measure table, so these figures should not be read as an official time-stamped election-night feed.
Does a close result automatically mean a recount?
No. The mandatory threshold compares the margin with 0.5% of the highest vote total in the contest, not with total ballots cast. The official canvass supplies the relevant totals. The measure filter’s “Within 3 pts” label is only a visual comparison with the passage threshold, not a recount classification.
